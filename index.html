1 AI 101: what do I need to know first?

No jargon, no hype. Ten minutes here and you will know enough to start. Each answer starts with the short version.

Last updated: 26 Oct 2026

What is AI, really?

Today's AI tools are software trained on huge numbers of examples (text, images, audio) that produce new text, images or answers from the patterns they learned.

They do not 'know' things the way a person does; they are very good at producing likely, useful-looking output. The free Elements of AI course points out that AI means different things to different people, so it helps to be specific: most people today mean chatbots like ChatGPT or Claude.

Source: Elements of AI, chapter 1

What is a chatbot like ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

A chat app that sits on top of a 'model', the trained AI that actually writes the replies.

You type or speak; the model answers. Companies release new models every few weeks, which is why the Latest models table below exists.

Source: Latest models table (lab sources)

Can AI be wrong?

Yes. AI can confidently invent facts, links or numbers that sound right.

Treat it like a fast, clever intern: great for drafts and ideas, but check anything important yourself, especially health, money and legal questions. Ask it for links and open them.

How do I write a good prompt?

Say who it should act as, what you want, who it is for and what format you need.

Example: 'Act as a friendly tutor. Explain inflation to a 14-year-old in 5 bullet points.' Then reply with 'shorter', 'more examples' or 'now as a table'.

Is it safe to paste personal information into a chatbot?

Don't paste passwords, bank details, medical records or other people's private information.

Check each app's settings for whether your chats are used for training. Claude's pricing page, for example, lists model training as opt-out on its consumer plans.

Source: Claude plans

Do I need to pay to use AI?

No. ChatGPT and Claude both have free plans, with usage limits.

ChatGPT's pricing page says the free version is available to everyone; Claude's Free plan is $0. Upgrade only when you keep hitting a limit doing something genuinely useful.

Source: ChatGPT plans · Claude plans

Which AI chatbot should a beginner start with?

Start with the free plan of ChatGPT or Claude; both work on web and mobile.

Both pricing pages list free chat on the web and phone apps, plus voice. Pick one, use it for a week on real tasks (an email, a plan, a summary), then try the other on the same task and compare.

Source: ChatGPT plans · Claude plans

3 What's new in AI this week?

Latest: Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen is in the Gemini app and Search for everyone (12 Nov 2026). What happened, and why it matters to you.

Last updated: 13 Nov 2026

  1. 12 Nov 2026

    Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen is in the Gemini app and Search for everyone

    Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster Gemini 4 model for everyday work, coding and short agent tasks. It is rolling out in the Gemini app and Google Search for all users; in the Gemini app, free users get it through automatic model selection when it is the right fit. Source: blog.google

    Why it matters: You may already be getting answers from a newer Google model in Search or the Gemini app, without changing any settings.

  2. 10 Nov 2026

    Gemini 4 Carbon is now available to paying users

    Google made Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest Gemini 4 model, available to paying developers through the Gemini API and to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Google Antigravity. It uses the same introductory API price as Gemini 4 Argon. Source: blog.google

    Why it matters: Google's most capable model is no longer limited to a small test group, if you pay for Ultra or the API.

  3. 10 Nov 2026

    Businesses can now claim a profile in ChatGPT

    OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Eligible businesses where ChatGPT is available can claim a profile in Business Hub, keep details and product feeds up to date, and see aggregated mention and click numbers. Eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses can also add a clearly labelled Business Agent. Source: openai.com

    Why it matters: If you run a small shop, you can start putting accurate information about it into ChatGPT; if you are comparing products, some follow-up chats may be with a business's own agent, which OpenAI says is labelled separately.

  4. 9 Nov 2026

    Anthropic launches Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2

    Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2, an upgrade of its model for the hardest technical work, and Claude Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model for vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences organisations. Fable 5.2 is available on Anthropic's platforms; it is not on the Free plan (Pro can use it with usage credits; Max and Ultra include some Fable use). Source: anthropic.com

    Why it matters: If you already pay for Claude, a stronger top model is now available for the toughest jobs.

  5. 6 Nov 2026

    Meta brings Wonder-style memory to Muse and Meta AI

    Meta says it has rolled out specialised versions of its existing models, trained with Muse Wonder, to people who use the Muse app and Meta AI. The update is meant to remember important details better and keep going when a task hits a snag. Source: research.meta.ai

    Why it matters: If you already use Meta AI or Muse, the assistant may now hold onto context and finish everyday tasks more reliably.

  6. 22 Oct 2026

    Anthropic hands its 'Agent Skills' standard to the Agentic AI Foundation

    Anthropic is donating Agent Skills, an open standard for teaching AI agents specific tasks, to the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation. It says more than 70 agent platforms, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code, have adopted it since December 2025. Source: anthropic.com

    Why it matters: Skills you set up in one AI tool are more likely to work in others.

  7. 21 Oct 2026

    OpenAI says its frontier models and Codex are coming to Google Cloud's Model Garden, bought through Google Cloud Marketplace. General availability is planned 'in the coming weeks'; Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner. Source: openai.com

    Meta calls it the most capable US open-weight model to date: 816B parameters (42B active), with a 1M-token context window. Source: research.meta.ai

4 What can I try with AI today?

Copy a prompt, paste it into a free chatbot, and see what happens. Each takes about five minutes.

Last updated: 26 Oct 2026

ChatGPT (Free) - Study mode

Ask it to teach you something

I want to understand how mortgages work in Ireland. Teach me step by step, ask me one question at a time, and check I understood before moving on.

ChatGPT's pricing page lists Study mode on the Free plan. Check anything Ireland-specific with an official source. Source: chatgpt.com

Any free chatbot

Fix an email you're dreading

Rewrite this email so it is polite but firm, under 120 words, and ends with one clear next step: [paste email]

Ask for 3 versions, then say which one you like and why.

Any free chatbot

Plan a week of dinners on a budget

Plan 5 weeknight dinners for 2 adults, under 30 minutes each, using ingredients from an Irish supermarket. Give me one combined shopping list grouped by aisle.

Do not trust its prices. Use it for the plan and the list only.

ChatGPT or Claude voice mode (Free, limited)

Have a spoken conversation

Let's practise a job interview for a [role]. Ask me one question at a time and give me feedback after each answer.

Both pricing pages list voice on their free plans (ChatGPT says 'limited'). Source: claude.com

Claude (Free) - Artifacts

Build a tiny app without coding

Make me a simple interactive tip calculator in euro that splits a bill between friends.

Claude's Free plan lists 'Create Artifacts'. Then try: 'add a dark mode'. Source: claude.com

Claude (Free) - web search

Make it show its sources

What changed in the latest AI model releases this month? Give me links to the official announcements for every claim.

Click the links. If one doesn't support the claim, don't use the claim. Source: claude.com

ChatGPT (Free) - vision (limited)

Turn a photo into notes

Here's a photo of my whiteboard. Turn it into tidy meeting notes with action items and owners.

Never upload photos of IDs, bank cards or other people's private info. Source: chatgpt.com

Log important emails to a spreadsheet automatically

Beginner⏱ 20 minZapier (Free)GmailGoogle Sheets
Show the steps
  1. Make a Google Sheet with columns: Date, From, Subject.
  2. In Zapier, create a Zap. Trigger: Gmail 'New email matching search' (e.g. from:bank OR subject:invoice).
  3. Action: Google Sheets 'Create spreadsheet row' and map Date, From and Subject.
  4. Test it, then turn it on. Zapier's free plan allows two-step Zaps, 100 tasks a month and checks every 15 minutes.
  5. If a step says 'Premium', it isn't on the free plan. Pick a different app.

Free-plan limits: zapier.com

Collect feedback with a form and get a weekly AI summary

Beginner⏱ 25 minTally (Free)Google SheetsChatGPT or Claude (Free)
Show the steps
  1. Build a short feedback form in Tally (3 to 5 questions).
  2. In the form's Integrations tab, connect Google Sheets. Every response becomes a new row (free on all Tally plans).
  3. Once a week, copy the new rows and paste them into a chatbot with: 'Summarise the top 3 themes, the most common complaint, and one quick fix.'
  4. Add the summary to a 'Weekly notes' tab so you can see trends.

Free-plan limits: tally.so

A reusable 'meeting notes to action items' assistant

Beginner⏱ 15 minClaude (Free) - Projects
Show the steps
  1. In Claude, create a Project called 'Meeting notes'. The Free plan allows up to 5 Projects.
  2. Add these instructions: 'When I paste notes or a transcript, return: 1) 3-line summary, 2) decisions, 3) action items as a table (owner, task, due date), 4) open questions.'
  3. After each meeting, paste your rough notes into a new chat inside the Project.
  4. Copy the action-item table into your to-do app or email.

Free-plan limits: claude.com

Build your own weekly news digest

Intermediate⏱ 40 minMake (Free)Google SheetsAny free chatbot
Show the steps
  1. Pick 3 to 5 sites you trust that have an RSS feed.
  2. In Make, create a scenario: an RSS 'Watch RSS Feed Items' module, then a Google Sheets 'Add a Row' module (map title, link and date). Those are the module names in Make's help for RSS and Google Sheets.
  3. Schedule it to run once a day. Make's free plan gives 1,000 credits a month, and most actions use 1 credit.
  4. On Fridays, paste the week's rows into a chatbot: 'Group these into themes and write a 5-bullet digest with the links.'

Free-plan limits: make.com

A personal FAQ helper for your small business or club

Beginner⏱ 30 minChatGPT (Free) - Projects
Show the steps
  1. Write your opening hours, prices, policies and common answers into one document.
  2. In ChatGPT, create a Project (listed on the Free plan) and add the document.
  3. Instruction: 'Answer customer questions using only this document. If the answer isn't there, say so and suggest they contact us.'
  4. Paste a customer's question in, review the draft, then send it yourself. Don't let it reply unsupervised.

Free-plan limits: chatgpt.com

Turn a job advert into a tailored application checklist

Beginner⏱ 15 minChatGPT or Gemini (Free)
Show the steps
  1. Copy the job advert (or just the requirements section) into a new chat in ChatGPT or Gemini.
  2. Underneath, add a short list of your relevant experience. Leave out private details: no PPS number, address, phone number or exact current salary unless you would be happy for a stranger at that company to see them.
  3. Use this prompt: 'Here is a job advert and a short list of my experience. Give me: 1) a checklist of the main requirements, marked as "I have this", "I can show something close", or "I don't have this" based only on what I wrote, 2) five things I should mention in an application, 3) five likely interview questions. If the advert doesn't mention something, say so rather than guessing.'
  4. Check every tick against the advert and your own experience before you use it.

Free-plan limits: chatgpt.com
