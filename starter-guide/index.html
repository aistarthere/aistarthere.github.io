Your first 5 things to try with AI

Pick one free chatbot, open a new chat, and paste the prompts below. Each task takes 5 to 10 minutes. Swap the [brackets] for your own details.

Download the PDF

Before you start: pick one free chatbot

Any of these has a free version. Sign in with an account you already have:

  • ChatGPT at chatgpt.com (has a free version)
  • Gemini at gemini.google.com (free with a Google account)
  • Copilot at copilot.microsoft.com (has a free version)

Don't paste passwords, bank details, medical records or other people's private information. Check anything important: AI can be wrong.

1 Fix an email you're dreading

Write a rough version in your own words, however clumsy, then paste it after this prompt.

Rewrite my draft email below so it's clear, polite and short. Keep my meaning and any dates or numbers exactly as they are. Give me two versions: one friendly, one more formal. Here's my draft:

2 Summarise a long email or document

Copy the text of a long email thread, report or letter. Paste it under this prompt.

Summarise the text below for me. Give me: 1) the main point in one sentence, 2) up to 5 bullet points with the key facts, 3) anything I need to do, with any dates or deadlines, 4) anything that's unclear or that I should ask about. Use plain English and quote the original wording for any number or date.

Then check the numbers and dates against the original.

3 Turn messy notes into a to-do list

Meeting scribbles, a voice-note transcript, a brain dump: paste it in as it is.

Turn my messy notes below into a clear to-do list. Group the tasks by topic, put the most urgent first, and add a deadline wherever my notes mention one. If something is vague, list it at the end as a question for me. My notes:

4 Plan a week of meals and a shopping list

Plan 5 simple weeknight dinners for [2 adults and 1 child]. Each should take under [30] minutes. We don't eat [mushrooms]. Reuse ingredients across meals so nothing goes to waste. Then give me one shopping list grouped by supermarket aisle.

Not happy with a meal? Reply "swap Wednesday for something with chicken".

5 Learn something, step by step

Anything you've been meaning to understand: a pension statement, a spreadsheet formula, a new hobby.

I want to understand [how a tracker mortgage works]. I'm a complete beginner. Explain it in plain English, one small step at a time. After each step, ask me a quick question to check I've got it before moving on.

Three habits that make every answer better

  1. Say who it's for. "For my landlord", "for a 10-year-old", "for my manager".
  2. Reply, don't restart. "Shorter", "warmer", "as a table" works better than a new chat.
  3. Check facts that matter. Names, numbers, dates, prices and anything legal or medical.
