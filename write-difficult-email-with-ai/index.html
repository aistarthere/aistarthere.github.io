How to write a difficult email with AI

You need to send an email you'd rather not write: a chase-up, a polite no, a complaint, or a firm request. A free AI chatbot can turn a rough draft into two clear versions, one friendly and one more formal. You still decide what to send.

What you need

  • A rough draft in your own words, however clumsy. If you don't have one yet, a few messy sentences about the situation will do.
  • One free chatbot. Each of these has a free plan: ChatGPT (OpenAI's plans page lists a Free plan and says the free version is available to everyone), Gemini (Google's plans page lists a free plan with a Google Account) or Copilot (Microsoft's plans page compares its paid plans with free use).
  • Five minutes, including a check before you send.

Before you paste: a 10-second privacy check

Whatever you paste goes to the company that runs the chatbot. Read your draft first and ask: would I be happy for a stranger at that company to see this? If not, don't paste it, or cut those parts out first.

  • Leave out: passwords, bank or card details, PPS or other ID numbers, medical details, and other people's private information.
  • Work email? Check your employer's rules before pasting anything from work into a personal AI account. Many workplaces don't allow it.
  • Swap names for roles if you like ("the landlord", "my manager"). The rewrite still works.

What each company says:

  • Google asks you not to enter confidential information you wouldn't want a human reviewer to see or Google to use to improve its services (Gemini Apps Privacy Hub).
  • Microsoft says you shouldn't give Copilot confidential or sensitive personal data you wouldn't want Microsoft to use as its privacy statement describes (Privacy FAQ for Microsoft Copilot; Microsoft notes this page covers the older Copilot app, and points users of the updated app to this overview).
  • OpenAI says that on ChatGPT Free, Plus or Pro, sharing your chats to improve its models is on by default. You can switch off "Improve the model for everyone" in Settings → Data controls, or use a Temporary chat, which isn't used to improve its models but may be kept for up to 30 days for safety (Data controls in ChatGPT).

The prompt (copy this)

This is the same prompt as task 1 in our starter guide. It asks for two versions and tells the chatbot not to change your meaning, dates or numbers.

Rewrite my draft email below so it's clear, polite and short. Keep my meaning and any dates or numbers exactly as they are. Give me two versions: one friendly, one more formal. Here's my draft:

Steps: ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot

The steps are the same in all three.

  1. Open the chatbot in your browser: chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com or copilot.microsoft.com. Sign in if it asks.
  2. Start a new chat, so older chats don't mix in.
  3. Paste the prompt above into the message box. Don't send it yet.
  4. Add your draft. Press Shift+Enter (or tap the return key on a phone) for a new line, then paste your rough email underneath. If you don't have a draft, write a few messy sentences about what happened, what you want, and any date or number that matters.
  5. Send it and read both versions.
  6. Follow up in the same chat if you need a tweak. You can paste one of these as a reply:
    • "Make the friendly one a bit firmer, but still polite."
    • "Make the formal one a bit softer."
    • "Shorten it to under 80 words."
    • "Keep my offer to be in after 5pm, and drop the weekend."

A worked example

This is a made-up situation, written to show the shape of a good rewrite. It is not a copy of a chatbot's actual reply, and it is not about a real person or address.

Imagine you need to chase a repair. A rough draft might look like this:

hi, just checking in about the leak under the kitchen sink you said you'd look at last week. it's still dripping and there's a water stain on the ceiling now. can you send someone this week? I'm around after 5pm on weekdays or anytime at the weekend. thanks

A friendly version might look like this:

Hi,

I'm following up on the leak under the kitchen sink. You said you'd look at it last week, but it's still dripping and there's now a water stain on the ceiling. Could you send someone this week? I'm around after 5pm on weekdays, or any time at the weekend.

Thanks

A more formal version might look like this:

Hello,

I'm writing to follow up on the leak under the kitchen sink, which you said you would inspect last week. The leak is still dripping, and a water stain has now appeared on the ceiling. Please arrange for someone to attend this week. I am available after 17:00 on weekdays, or any time at the weekend.

Kind regards

Notice what stayed the same: the leak, "last week", the water stain, "this week", and the after-5pm or weekend availability. The chatbot's job is to tidy the wording, not to add new facts or drop the ones you care about.

Check before you send

AI rewrites are usually clear, but they can soften a point you meant to keep, add a promise you didn't make, or get a date wrong. Before you copy anything into your own email:

  • Dates and numbers. The prompt asks it to keep these exactly as they are. Search your draft for each one and make sure the rewrite matches.
  • Your meaning. If you wanted to say no, the rewrite should still say no. If you wanted a date for a reply, that ask should still be there.
  • Facts it may have added. Cross out any sentence you didn't put in the draft: an apology you didn't mean, a deadline you didn't set, or a "as we agreed" that wasn't agreed.
  • The name and greeting. Add the real name yourself. Don't send a placeholder.
  • Anything important (money, legal, medical, a complaint you'll have to stand over): read every line in your own voice. Use the rewrite as a draft, not the final word. You are the one sending it.

Make it even more useful

  • Say who it's for: "This is for my landlord" or "This is for a client I don't know well."
  • Ask for a subject line if you want one: "Give me a short, plain subject line too."
