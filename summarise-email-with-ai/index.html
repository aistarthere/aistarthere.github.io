How to summarise a long email with AI (free, 2 minutes)

A long email thread lands and you need the gist: what happened, what you have to do, and by when. A free AI chatbot can pull that out in about two minutes. You need one prompt, copy and paste, and a quick check at the end.

What you need

  • The email (or thread) open on your screen.
  • One free chatbot. Each of these has a free plan: ChatGPT (OpenAI's plans page lists a Free plan), Gemini (Google's plans page lists a €0 plan with a Google Account) or Copilot (Microsoft's plans page compares its paid plans with free use).
  • Two minutes.

Before you paste: a 10-second privacy check

Whatever you paste goes to the company that runs the chatbot. Read the email first and ask: would I be happy for a stranger at that company to see this? If not, don't paste it, or cut those parts out first.

  • Leave out: passwords, bank or card details, PPS or other ID numbers, medical details, and other people's private information.
  • Work email? Check your employer's rules before pasting anything from work into a personal AI account. Many workplaces don't allow it.
  • Swap names for roles if you like ("the landlord", "my manager"). The summary still works.

What each company says:

  • Google asks you not to enter confidential information you wouldn't want a human reviewer to see or Google to use to improve its services (Gemini Apps Privacy Hub).
  • Microsoft says you shouldn't give Copilot confidential or sensitive personal data you wouldn't want Microsoft to use as its privacy statement describes (Privacy FAQ for Microsoft Copilot; Microsoft notes this page covers the older Copilot app, and points users of the updated app to this overview).
  • OpenAI says that on ChatGPT Free, sharing your chats to improve its models is on by default. You can switch off "Improve the model for everyone" in Settings → Data controls, or use a Temporary chat, which isn't used to improve its models but may be kept for up to 30 days for safety (Data controls in ChatGPT).

The prompt (copy this)

This is the same prompt as task 2 in our starter guide. It asks for the main point, the key facts, your to-dos and anything unclear.

Summarise the text below for me. Give me: 1) the main point in one sentence, 2) up to 5 bullet points with the key facts, 3) anything I need to do, with any dates or deadlines, 4) anything that's unclear or that I should ask about. Use plain English and quote the original wording for any number or date.

Steps: ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot

The steps are the same in all three.

  1. Open the chatbot in your browser: chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com or copilot.microsoft.com. Sign in if it asks.
  2. Start a new chat, so older chats don't mix in.
  3. Paste the prompt above into the message box. Don't send it yet.
  4. Add the email. Press Shift+Enter (or tap the return key on a phone) for a new line, then paste the email text underneath. Copy the whole thread, oldest to newest, if replies matter.
  5. Send it and read the summary.
  6. Follow up in the same chat if you need more: "Make it shorter", "Who is waiting on me?" or "Draft a short reply that says yes to the Thursday meeting".

Very long thread? Paste it in two halves and say "Here's part 1 of 2, wait for part 2 before summarising".

Check the summary before you act on it

AI summaries are usually good, but they can drop a detail or get one wrong. Spend 30 seconds checking:

  • Numbers and dates. The prompt asks it to quote the original wording for these, so search the email for each one (Ctrl+F, or Cmd+F on a Mac) and make sure it matches.
  • Your to-dos. Scan the email for "please", "can you" and "by". Anything asked of you should be in point 3.
  • Who said what. In long threads the chatbot can mix up people. Check any action it gives to a named person.
  • Anything important (money, legal, medical): read the original wording yourself. Use the summary as a map, not the final word.

Make it even more useful

  • Say who it's for: "Summarise this for my partner, who wasn't on the thread."
  • Ask for a format: "As a table with columns: task, who, deadline."
  • Turn it into actions: paste the to-dos into the "messy notes" prompt from the starter guide (task 3).
